About the book

Every dead hand in the Tower is pardoned at dawn. Darin Sivel is the one who begs to stay.

Darin is sixteen, a docker's son, and the only person alive who can stop a spell with a single word. Each time, the word takes heat from his body. His right hand is white to the wrist, and his heart skips every morning before the bells.

Talior Escrin rules the Tower now, with half his face gone grey from the day Darin stopped a spell in his mouth. He empties the cells of the convict servants and fills the halls with children from the Fish Market and the Stonecutters' Quarter. The halls feed whatever lies under the Tower, and the new apprentices double the portion. Talior is right about one thing: whoever can stop a spell can stop every mage in the kingdom.

The word works only for people who have spent years beside live magic, and the pardon sends them far from it. Outside the gate, the Crown is waiting for a Tower with nobody left in it who can speak the word.

If Darin's heart gives out, the word dies with him or ends up in a king's hand. Saving it means teaching it, as his father once threatened to, and putting the people he cares about back beside the magic that cripples them.

Who gets to say stop?
