About the book

The virus in five soldiers' veins makes them nearly immortal and keeps them hungry for blood. They have been declared prey, they are starving, and the only plasma store lies in a colony that will be burned from orbit in six days.

Lieutenant Anika Reyes leads the Pack, a platoon her own army created and then declared outlaw. On the second day without plasma, her doctor says to her: "You tell me you've eaten, but I never saw you drink." "I ate," Anika answers.

The store is on Heron, a mining colony of six hundred and twelve people that has been silent for eleven days. Its miners vanish as "industrial accidents," the survivors hate "the blues," their name for the infected soldiers, and beneath the shafts an alien nest is stirring.

A mercenary ship drops into orbit with a contract for five whole bodies: two million one hundred thousand credits apiece, nothing for ash. Their leader, Korda, is seventy-one, and as a boy he watched the blues take his home. He does not lie, and he does not exaggerate.

Anika's scout hears every heartbeat in the tunnels and is one bad night away from losing the man inside him. Anika tells no one how little she drinks. Every decision she makes will be paid for by one of her people, and the colony she has to bring out before the fire won't take her hand.

Who pays more for the whole body than for the ashes, and why will no one be looking at Heron?

Book Two of The Blue Pack. Military sci-fi about a squad that saves the people who hate it.
