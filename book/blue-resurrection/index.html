About the book

Humanity is losing the war, and its only weapon is a virus that makes a soldier stronger, harder to kill, and hungry for blood. Lieutenant Anika Reyes says "yes" on behalf of her entire platoon.

Standing before the list of the dead from a vanished fleet, one of the five says "yes" first, for her brother's name. Anika has already signed for all five of them, without waiting for the others.

In the fifth year of the war, humanity is losing to the Architects, an alien swarm that does not slaughter but collects. So the command resurrects the weapon that once nearly destroyed it: the virus of the "blues." The platoon lives on a stealth corvette that is also a cell, and its teacher is De Las, the last living blue.

Power costs memories, and the command counts Anika's people as an expense. Every decision she makes costs the others more than they know.

The admiral who chose them has a deadline from the politicians above him: a miracle or a tribunal. He needs proof that the weapon works, and Anika needs a way to give it without spending her platoon. Their first mission leads to a station that has been silent for nine days.

She does not yet understand why the army that calls De Las a hero has kept him in chains for sixty years, or why he says nothing about it.

Book One of The Blue Pack. Military science fiction about duty, control, and the cost of power.
