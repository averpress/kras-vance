Seven years of sweeping up after wizards, and one word that could hang him.

Darin Sivel is sixteen, a dockhand's son, caught stealing from a warehouse and sentenced to serve the Conclave of the Vaults as a dead hand: grey shirt, cloth band on the wrist, no rights, no voice. Nobody in the Tower of Roots looks at the people who carry the buckets. That suits Darin. He means to sweep, keep his head down, and outlast the sentence.

Then the library catches fire. An apprentice's spell tears loose from his hand, and Darin does what any docker does when a load slips the hook. He shouts the word his father shouted on the quay. The flame stops mid-air and sets into red glass.

Now the practice halls keep failing. Lanterns blind apprentices. Frost locks a novice's hands. Every time, the first one on the scene is the servant with the bucket, and Talior Eskrin, the apprentice who caught Darin in the warehouse, wants a name to blame before his father's letter arrives. The Masters will hang a dead hand for a forbidden word. Dead hands cannot testify. Talior is hunting for a free man who heard it.

Darin has a crew of his own: a sweeper who knows every cracked flagstone, a thirteen-year-old thief who cannot keep a secret, and a stonecutter's daughter who speaks to him only where no one is watching.

The next accident is already on its way.
